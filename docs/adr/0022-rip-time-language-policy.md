# Rip-time language policy: guaranteed audio, unfiltered subtitles

Three films in the library are silent — Battle Royale (2000), Last Train Home (2009), Unknown Pleasures (2002) — because MakeMKV's selection string filtered audio to Danish/English/original-language, and `iso2b` mapped only ten languages, passing TMDB's two-letter codes (`ja`, `zh`, …) through to a matcher that expects three-letter ones (mapped codes do match: a `fra` selection keeps `fre` tracks — MakeMKV normalizes). The `single` token saved sole-audio-track discs only; discs with two or more unmatched audio tracks went silent, and nothing between rip and library checked. Subtitles shared the same filter, so anything the household might have wanted had to survive rip time first — X-Men Days of Future Past staged zero subs twice on the same disc before a third rip caught dan+eng.

Audio is now guaranteed, not filtered: the selection widens with the title's scanned audio languages whenever none intersect the keep set (dan/eng) or the original language, and a post-rip audio gate hard-fails any staged main or episode holding zero audio streams (extras are unchecked — galleries legitimately carry no audio). The remedy for a trip is the one-shot `-K/--keep-all` re-rip, which stages every audio language regardless. Subtitles invert the policy: every track rips, in every language — subtitle tracks are MB-scale and the feature encode embeds none anyway (`zzz` lang-list trick) — and filtering to the keep set happens once, at the subtitle pass. Subless outcomes warn and never fail: the rip-time subtitle shortfall warning (staged subs vs what the scan promised) and the transcode-time "(no dan/eng)" note cover the X-Men DoFP shape, while discs that genuinely carry no subtitle streams (Skibet i Skilteskoven, The Atomic Cafe) stay quiet. The warn/fail asymmetry is deliberate: silence is unwatchable, missing subtitles are watchable.

Rejected: ripping all audio (HD tracks add gigabytes — the `havemulti|havecore` dedupe exists for a reason); an original-language subtitle fallback at transcode (a `ger.srt` sidecar is useless to this household); a soft audio gate (a silent film must never reach the library). The per-type selection syntax (`+sel:subtitle`, `audio&(...)`) must be verified on a junk disc before it's trusted — the `custom.mmcp.xml` comment calling those tokens invalid is stale forum folklore, but `&` needs `&amp;` escaping in the profile XML, and the scan-driven language union is the fallback either way. Four library items get hand-deleted and re-ripped under the fixed code: the three silent films and Regret to Inform (audible but subtitleless in a language the household reads subtitles in — unlike the Danish-language Danskernes egen historie discs, which stay as-is).
